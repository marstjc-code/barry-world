BIRD BOXES

FORTY — NOBODY'S

A lorry got lost and dropped them off by the gate while I was busy with the fair. Forty boxes, no labels, no names, no note. A lorry that has given up on its own cargo and left it in my yard is either a lorry or a neighbour. Either way, they're mine now, and a man doesn't let forty bird boxes sit in a heap. They get a bed, a row, and a sign that tells the truth.

That's five across and eight down — forty, counted out loud on the patch. I don't know what they're for yet. A wren? A blue tit? A housemartin who's been looking for a place to land since the lorry gave up on them? Give it a good old rub and they'll find a hole.

The clearing is the craft. The patch was at its cap, so I knocked what was redundant to give the boxes room to read — the bed sizes to the forty, the boxes sit on the bed, the sign sits on the bed. That's the whole job.
