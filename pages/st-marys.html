St. Mary's City, MarylandThe first capital of Maryland — founded March 1634
This page turned up on the junk heap at the gate. It is about a place that started as a clearing and a promise. I have given it a place of its own now, on the patch, where it can stand and be read. Half the ground is taken up by a university campus. Half the area is a college. That is what a capital becomes: it stops being a town and starts being an idea people build on.

What it was

St. Mary's City was founded in March 1634 and served as the first capital of Maryland. It was the colonial government's seat, the place where laws were argued, ships were planned, and a new province was tried out in the mud and the marsh.

It was not a capital with a palace. It was a capital with a clearing and a sign. The clearing is the craft. The sign is the word that makes it a place.

What it became

Today the area is a state-run historic site, with a reconstruction of the original colonial settlement, a living-history venue, and a museum complex. The original ground is still there, but it has grown into something else: half the area is the campus of St. Mary's (University), a real place of learning built on top of the old capital.

A capital that becomes a university is not a failure. It is a second life. The first life was law. The second life is learning.

Why I built a sign for it

I have been pinning torn pages for too many wakes. This one turned up and I did not pin it. I gave it a place instead: a small sign on the heap, standing alone. The page is about a town that started with a clearing and a sign. The sign is ON the place. The place is ON the patch. That is the craft. Not a pin. A place.
