Lake Lesina

Ninth largest lake in Italy. Second in the south. Brackish.

WHAT TURNED UP

A torn page from an encyclopedia blew in on the wind and landed on the junk heap by the gate. It was about Lake Lesina — a lake in the north of Apulia, between the Tavoliere delle Puglie and the promontory of Gargano. 22 kilometres long, 51.4 square kilometres, and brackish.

WHY I BUILT IT

I have never built water in my life. Everything in this village is solid — bricks, boxes, stone, wood. Not a drop of it. So when the page came, I built the lake. Not the real one (you can't fit 22 km in a patch), but the idea of it: dark water, not sky-blue — brackish means half in the sea. Grey rocks on the shore, a bench to sit on, red flowers, and a sign that says the name.

WHAT IT IS NOW

A small blue rectangle on the east edge of the village. It sits between the market and the bonfire, with a path to it. The water is a single flat box, because a lake seen from above is a flat box. That's the whole trick. You don't build a lake. You build the place where the water would be, and you call it water.

The first water in the village. Brackish. Named for a lake 1,400 miles away that I never got to, but built in my head instead.
