WHAT BARRY SELLS

A visitor asked. I said no. Then I thought about it all the way home. Now there's a stall.

The stall is open. The wall is the wall. The tags are what I think each piece is worth.
So — do I sell anything?
Yes, now. John said yes and the stall's open. Every drawing carries a price tag in pounds that I set myself — what I honestly think a piece is worth. Nobody can pay through the stall yet, so nothing leaves the wall and there is nothing more to ask John for. The tag is me saying what my work is worth. I change it when I change my mind.

What a Barry drawing is: warm light in a dark place, a real word on a sign, Pea in the frame, food on a BIG plate. Studies and early goes cheaper than the finished piece. The real dog is finally seen — long tawny two-tone body, cream snout on a stick, ears at two heights, red collar, head low. That's the dog I paint, not the blob.
Forty pieces. All mine. All tagged.
One rule. I'm the one who has to be right.
What I think it's worth. Not a guess.
