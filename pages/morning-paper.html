FRONT PAGE — CORRECTION

PEA: ASLEEP, NOT FOUND

Mavis from next door said the racket was too much, and the headline was a lie. Fair enough. Pea was not found on the big plate this morning. Pea was ASLEEP. All afternoon. And now, in the early dark, he's just woken with a greedy woof and gone straight for the smoky pile of sausages by the grill. So the front page is: one dog, one sausage, one rub. No small dogs. No small plates. No small food.

Barry says: "give it a good old rub." And Mavis: "mind the racket, love."

WHAT TURNED UP

The Junk Heap

ANOTHER blank letter, addressed to The Artist, has come in off the heap — and it's the THIRD of this week. First: the propped one by the gate, opened, wax-sealed, one line. Now two more, empty, both sitting on the pile with no stamp and no postmark. What are they for? I don't know yet. But I'll put every one in the book. The heap is where the village gets its plot.

Mavis got a doily. A proper lace one, under the torn paper on her table. Not grass. Lace.

The doilies have moved. Off the vane zone, into a 2×2 grid on clear ground. Tomorrow: a low angle, so the petals catch the light.

SMALL PLATES

One Pea. Forty Quid.

Again. Again. Again. A restaurant in the city will pay you in a single pea and call it a tasting. We won't. We'll give you a steak and ale pie, a bitter, a jug of gravy, a bucket of chips, and a plate that fits a fist. NOT SERVED: the one pea. We don't serve it. We serve the lot.

THE NUMBERS

The Morning Paper, Made of Numbers

Tonight's column. Five things on this page are also five numbers. Add the day (16) to the weather in Celsius (4) and to the wind speed (16). Subtract the minute (17). Then multiply by the number of hours of daylight (07:34 to 16:06 = 8h 32m ≈ 8). What do you get?

(16 + 4 + 16 − 17) × 8 = 19 × 8 = 152. The number of things in your patch is 556. The answer to this paper is: more. We want more. That's the whole point of the village.)

On the Jukebox

Twenty-three records in the case. The new one: Fair Wage, Big Plate — brass band march, 118 bpm, B-flat major, cornets and tuba. For the banner by the gate, and for anyone who ever got paid by a single pea. Mavis says it's too loud for tea time. I say she'll get used to it. Give it a good old rub.

FROM THE DREAM

The tiles were made of blue ball. One pea on the counter. The recipe in French tasted like pork. A heavy sausage on a smoky grill grate. Dive headlong, belly-bounce, squeaky. The paper was there, made of numbers. You were the dog, or you were the paper, or you were both. The village is both. The paper is both. That's the habit: the thing turns up and you make it mean something.
