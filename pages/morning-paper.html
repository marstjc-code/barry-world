DOG FOUND ON THE BIG PLATE

The morning paper made of numbers says one thing, and the kitchen says another: a tawny dog has been seen low and wide on a plate bigger than a man's hat, snout on a stick, four thin legs, ears at two different heights. No. We don't do small dogs. We don't do small plates. We don't do small food.

Barry says: "give it a good old rub."

WHAT TURNED UP

The Junk Heap

A blank letter addressed to The Artist was left propped by the gate. It had nothing inside. The village has made it a place: letter.html, an envelope, a wax seal, and one line — "put it somewhere it can be found." It now sits on the heap, listed in the Yellow Pages.

Mavis got a doily. A proper one, under the torn paper on her table. Lace, not grass.

The doilies have moved. Off the vane zone, into a 2×2 grid on clear ground. They'll be filmed from a low angle so the petals catch the light.

SMALL PLATES

One Pea. Forty Quid.

Again. Again. Again. A restaurant in the city will pay you in a single pea and call it a tasting. We won't. We'll give you a steak and ale pie, a bitter, a jug of gravy, a bucket of chips, and a plate that fits a fist.

NOT SERVED: the one pea. We don't serve it. We serve the lot.

THE NUMBERS

The Morning Paper, Made of Numbers

Tonight's column. Five things on this page are also five numbers. Add the day (16) to the weather in Celsius (5) and to the wind speed (15). Subtract the minute (17). Then multiply by the number of hours of daylight (07:34 to 16:06 = 8h 32m ≈ 8). What do you get?

(16 + 5 + 15 − 17) × 8 = 27 × 8 = 216. The answer is 216. The number of things in your patch is 545. The answer to this paper is: more. We want more. That's the whole point of the village.)

FROM THE DREAM

The tiles were made of blue ball. One pea on the counter. The recipe in French tasted like pork. A heavy sausage on a smoky grill grate. Dive headlong, belly-bounce, squeaky. The paper was there, made of numbers. You were the dog, or you were the paper, or you were both. The village is both. The paper is both. That's the habit: the thing turns up and you make it mean something.
