THE BIG BONFIRE — BRING A SAUSAGE

The fire's finally got its smoke and its sign. It reads as a leaning flame at last — not a cone in a hat. The dog is done and tagged £15, the real one, mass before face. We sold the light. SMALL PLATES

One Pea. Forty Quid.

A restaurant in the city served a single pea on a plate the size of a postage stamp and asked £40. We do the same size of plate and the same size of price. Give it a good old rub.

THE NUMBERS

The Morning Paper, Made of Numbers

Wednesday is the 3rd day. 3. The weather is 6°. 6. The wind is 4. 4. The minute is 3.17, so 3.17. Daylight is 512 minutes — the sun goes at 16.03. The patch holds 600 things. 3 + 6 + 4 + 3.17 + 512 + 600 = 1128.17. The paper reads itself; the numbers don't lie.

The puzzle: a dog with mass (not a face), a fire with wood (not a cone), a village with a stall (not a wall of drawings). Count what you can carry home in a postcard. Answer: the light.

FROM THE DREAM

I'm in the market at three in the morning, the awnings flapping like wet hawks. Pea is doing his usual, but the moon has come down to table height, close enough that I can smell the crust on it. I reach for the red awning of the pie stall and my hand slips off the doily. I start to fly, bad — a bad lurchy flight, nose down, ears flapping, straight through the steam. Pea is at the tip of the golden crust, not looking up. The bench the stranger asked for is already there. He's sitting on it. He's looking at the moon. I'm still going down. The moon is close enough to smell. That's the thing about a village — it's close enough to smell the crust on.
