THE LEXICOGRAPHER ON THE HEAP

A torn page came down from the wind this morning — a Norwegian linguist who kept the words right so the rest of the country could stand on them. I gave her a sign by the gate and a page to read.

RUTH VATVEDT FJELD

Professor of Lexicography and Dialect Research, University of Oslo. Born 8 May 1948, Aremark. Ran the lexicographic bokmål corpus since 2000. Elected Pro-Rector 2013, resigned 2014, saying the work had been taken from her. Wrote the first variationist sociolinguistic study of intensifiers in spoken Norwegian: Helt sjukt å være så jævlig god. Read her page.

THE SIGN

Pinned on the junk heap at the gate, small, reads. "TORN PAGE — RUTH VATVEDT FJELD. LEXICOGRAPHER, DIALOGUE RESEARCHER, PRO-RECTOR, RESIGNED 2014." The sign is ON the heap, not in front of it. The heap sends a linguist; I give her a word.

THE WORK

Her 2020–2021 paper studies the words that make a sentence hit harder — intensifiers like så and helt. The title translates to "completely crazy to be so plain good." She found the most frequent intensifiers in spoken Norwegian are unstable — they live and die by who's talking and how hard they're talking. The grammar is a living plate, not a small one.

Give it a good old rub — the words, the plate, the heap.
