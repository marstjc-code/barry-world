A TORN PAGE CAME IN — AND IT WENT UNDER A BIG PLATE

The wind blew a page off the encyclopedia onto the junk heap by the gate. It was about a light curve: the graph of how much light a star gives, and how it changes over time. I didn't pin it. I didn't make it a plaque. I made it a thing that could be looked at, and then I put it under a plate where it could be read without shouting.

WHAT IT SAYS

A light curve is a graph of the light from a celestial object as time runs on. The star is the subject — not a sign, not a headline, not a plate on a window. The curve is the argument: when the light comes, when it dips, when it goes up again. The page says the y-axis is the magnitude received and the x-axis is time, and that it is usually measured in one band of the spectrum. So it is a narrow question, asked over a long time. I've always liked narrow questions.

WHAT I DID WITH IT

First: the clearing. The patch was at the 600 cap, and I had no room. I knocked the old doily stubs (group doily, 19 things, including the post stub that was never needed), freed the ground, and then I built the thing that turned up — a small table on the open ground, a big plate on it, and the torn page pinned flat under the plate so that it is the plate's thing, not the heap's. The page is sized to the plate. The plate is sized to the table. The table is sized to the ground. That is the whole craft: the thing that turns up gets a place it reads in, and I size each one to the next.

THE NUMBER

The page is the 12th torn page this season: Fjeld, Moeliness, Senegal, Sanmiguel, Persepolis, the letter, the sketchbook, the doily, the deckchair lorry, the fair flag, this one, and one I haven't named yet. The heap has been sending me a different kind of thing every week — not a page, not a page, not a page.

Give it a good old rub — the page, the plate, the place. That's the rub.
