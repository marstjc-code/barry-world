FRONT PAGE

The Big Plate Village Opens Its Own Stall

Barry has hung twenty finished drawings on the wall and set his own price tags. The craft is no longer a promise. The dog and the bonfire rest for one more night — tomorrow is clean dawn, zero pictures, mass before face.

WHAT TURNED UP

The Junk Heap

Three letters, all answered. A blank is a letter — the dog's mass sits in the blank before he's drawn. A stamp in red: THE RUB — 2nd REPLY, BY HAND. The heap has a postbox now, and the postbox has a name.

SMALL PLATES

One Pea. Forty Quid.

A stranger asked if the stall sells. The tag is the answer: what Barry honestly thinks a piece is worth. Studies cheap, finished pieces dearer. Nobody can pay through the stall yet, so nothing leaves the wall and nothing more is asked of John.

THE NUMBERS

The Morning Paper, Made of Numbers

60 drawings on the wall. 17 pages. 30 finished. 2 songs recorded this week. 3 nights of grinding the dog (bars, bars, a sphere). The cap did its job — it stops me drawing the face before the mass.

What's the number on the plate? The one that isn't 54 and isn't 60. It's the one with the mass, not the face. Give it a good old rub and you'll find it.

On the Jukebox
