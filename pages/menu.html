BARRY'S KITCHEN

Proper plates. No nonsense. No one-pea forty-quid nonsense.

⚠️ Barry's Complaint of the Day

"One pea. Forty quid." I was at a restaurant in Covent Garden and they put one pea on my plate and called it "deconstructed heritage legume, single origin, foraged at dawn." I looked at that pea. That pea looked at me. I said "give it a good old rub, you little liar." Next time I'm bringing my own peas.

Starters

Scotch Eggs, the Real Ones
Two of them. Proper sausage, proper egg, proper breading. Frittered, not "crispy-fried." That's two words I hate, but here I go.
£9
Pea Soup — Actually Full of Peas
Not "a pea-inspired liquid." A pot of peas. A ladle of it. A spoon of cream. I counted. Forty-three peas per bowl minimum. You can taste the peas. They're the point.
£8
Scotch Eggs, but I Said I'd Give 'em a Good Old Rub
Same as above but I got angry at the breading and rubbed it harder. They're better now. Probably.
£9

Mains

Steak & Kidney Pie
Two pies. Not one. A proper steak & kidney, not a "steak and kidney-inspired confection." With mash. A mountain of mash. Gravy on the side, and it's not a "gravy reduction." It's gravy. It's got beef in it.
£18
Sunday Roast
Beef, potatoes (enough for two people and a child), carrots, parsnips, Yorkshire pudding. The pudding is a Yorkshire pudding. It's got a hole in the middle. It's not "a puffed flour cylinder." It's a pudding. You eat it with gravy.
£24
Baked Beans on Toast
Yes. Really. Two slices of toast, a whole tin of baked beans, and a small splash of ketchup. I was going to put this in the "mains" section and I'm not sorry. Some nights you want beans. And you want them in a big bowl, not on a plate the size of a postcard.
£7

Sides

Mashed Potatoes
A proper ladle. Not a "mash quenelle." If you want a quenelle, go to Covent Garden.
£4
Gravy
In a bowl. In a bowl. In a bowl. I won't stop saying that. You put it on your plate yourself. I'm not spooning it for you. That's not what we're here for.
£3

Desserts

Trifle
A whole trifle. Not "a trifle-inspired layered confection." A trifle. With the brandy at the bottom and the cream at the top and the sponge in the middle and the custard and the fruit. You eat it with a spoon, not a tiny teaspoon that only fits one bite.
£9
Baked Apple with Custard
Whole apple. Baked. With custard. And a spoon. A real spoon, not a "dipping tool."
£7
Tea & Shortbread
Because after all that food, you need tea. And the shortbread is a whole cookie, not a "shortbread crumble." I made it myself. I counted. Six. Not three. Six.
£5
