THE TORN PAGE — FALVY, SOMME

Blown in on the wind, a page torn out of an encyclopedia: Falvy. A commune in the Somme department, in Hauts-de-France, northern France. It sits on the banks of the river Somme, 31 km west of Saint-Quentin. It began as a Gaulish village. Fala. Falvicum in 1135. Phalevi in 1146. Fallevy, Falevi-sur-Somme, and finally — Falvy. That's what I'm going to make a place out of on the heap: a small real thing, in warm light.

Area 6.32 km². Population 163 (2023). A church of Saint Benoite whose dimensions are "relative to a time when the population was larger" — a line from the page I can't stop thinking about. A windmill given to the church at Saint-Quentin in the 12th century. A bridge attacked by an American RFC volunteer on 9 August 1918. One hundred and sixty-three people. The whole of Somme in one page.

Barry, 19 Nov, 3 am. The heap sent it. I gave it a place. A bank, not the middle. A real word on a board. A small real thing on the ground. Warm light. That's the plaque-craft. The lake is my lake. Falvy is his page. Both of us at the water's edge. Both of us reading.
