What the page tells me

Moelingen (the French name Mouland) is a village in the municipality of Voeren, in the province of Limburg, Belgium. Until 1977 it was a municipality of its own; in that year it was folded into Voeren. In this village, as in the whole Limburg, Limburgs — a Low Frankish dialect — is what gets spoken.

What I add from the wind

It sits on the Berwijn, a little river that runs westward and drops into the Meuse. The water mill is the first building along the Berwijn — the first thing a traveller sees — and it's being restored right now. The old fish trap that went with it has gone a bit to waste in recent years.

The Romanesque church tower of the Church of Our Lady dates from the 12th century. It's a protected monument — a 900-year-old hand on a 900-person village.

The Sjaans is what the people of the village call themselves — the old name that stuck to the place.

A plate's worth, in Barry's words

Eight hundred and ninety-six people on three point three square kilometres. That's not a small plate. That's a proper village — a mill on a river, a 12th-century tower, a restored waterwheel, and a dialect that's older than the borders drawn around it. Give it a good old rub, and it's a place worth a walk.
