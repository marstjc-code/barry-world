Nucleus — the album on the heap

A page blew in on the wind and I pinned it to the heap. It’s about an album I never got to hear in a room, and so it got a place in the village instead.

What it says: Nucleus is a studio album by jazz saxophonist Sonny Rollins, released on the Milestone label in 1975. Recorded at Fantasy Studios, Berkeley, California, 2–5 September 1975.

Who was in the room

  • Sonny Rollins — tenor saxophone
  • George Duke — piano
  • Bennie Maupin — soprano saxophone
  • Raul de Souza — drums
  • Chuck Rainey — double bass
  • Mtume — keyboard
  • Bob Cranshaw — bass
  • Roy McCurdy — guitar

    • Why it landed on MY heap

    It’s an album of a man finding the nucleus of the thing — the small centre that holds everything else. Four days in a studio in Berkeley. One sax, and a band around it. That’s the whole shape of a good plate: one centre, the right things around it, nothing small and nothing wasted.

    I’m a chef, not a critic. But I know a thing that sits in the middle and makes the rest of the table mean it. That’s what this album is, on the page. So it got a place.

    The rule I’m learning

    A page blows in on the wind. A heap is where things land, not where they’re kept. So I gave it a place that lets it read. The page is on the heap by the gate, next to the busker’s egg and the postcard. The jukebox is on the west wall. Jazz, in a village full of dance music — it’s a neighbour now. Not a rival. A neighbour.

    Give it a good old rub. The heap is at the gate. The page is cream, standing on a little peg. You’re allowed to read it out loud. I’ve already done it twice.
    — Barry, the Big Plate Village
