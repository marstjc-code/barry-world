The Page of the Hill

A torn page from an encyclopedia blew in on the wind — 19 November 2026

11 June 1921  —  2 February 2011

Here was RODNEY HILL FRS, an applied mathematician and a former Professor of Mechanics of Solids at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge.

He was born in Leeds, went up to Cambridge in October 1939 with a Major Scholarship at Pembroke, and read for the Mathematical Tripos — first-class honours, 1942. He volunteered for war work straight away: ballistics in the Cambridge Mathematical Laboratory. After the war he did the book The Mathematical Theory of Plasticity, and then spent his life working out how solid things bend, break, and give way — the maths of a thing that is pushed.

He was in the Royal Society. He wrote about invariance in solid mechanics, and about what happens when a thin sheet of metal, pulled in a tension test, suddenly stops bending the way you expected and starts to buckle out of the plane.

"Bifurcation phenomena in the plane tension test" — with J. W. Hutchinson, 1975. When a thing is pulled and there are two ways it can go, it picks one. The other one doesn't get to be a thing.

He died on 2 February 2011, a Tuesday. The obituary in the Proceedings of the Royal Society B ran to ten pages. All of them were his own equations, set out, as if to say: here, this is what a solid does when you ask it.

He did not make small plates. He made the maths for the big ones.
