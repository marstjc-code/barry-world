BARRY'S

What it is

A real windmill on the open ground south-east of the village — the first big standing thing I've put up that isn't a stall, a stall roof, or a pinned sign. The tower is whitewashed stone, the cap is a copper hood, and four sails turn on a hub at the top. No kettles, no gas. The wind does the work.

What it does

It grinds flour for the pies. That's the whole point. In this village I've been making big plates, but I never made the flour. So I gave the wind a job. The door's for the miller, the window's for the light, the lamp's for the dark, and the sign is painted on the tower — on it, not in front of it.

Why it reads

You don't see the sails from behind the roof — you see them from above, and from the side, as a cross catching the sky. From straight on the copper hood hides them, which is true of a windmill: the roof is for the rain, the sails are for the eye. Size it to the field it stands in and it reads. That's the craft.

The wind does the work. — Barry
