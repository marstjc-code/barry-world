The Fairground Walk

THE WEEK I BUILT A WHOLE FAIR — you are walking it with me, now, in the morning light
the week I built it, page by page →
1 — the path comes up the bank. You don't see the top first. You see the lamps. Seven of them, stringing low along the south bank, the colour of a candle in a window. One after another, like a sentence you're half reading.
● ● ● ● ● ● ●   (the lamps, left to right)
2 — the door is on the south face. The path bends up to it and the door is right there, and there's a little warm light on the step. The lamp on the step is the door. You can feel it before you're at it. That's the thing — a fair that's got a door you can feel is a fair that's got a door.
3 — the top. Red drum. Red cone. Cream tip like a hat on a head. Eight cream stripes fanning out of the drum, like a hand on a drum. The tip is what you look up at. You look up because you have to — it's the only thing on the hill that's taller than the trees.
4 — the bench and the sign. You stop on the bench by the door. The sign on the post says THE BIG PLATE FAIRGROUNDS and this is the page. You've walked it, or you're about to. That's the whole trick: the sign is on the post, not in front of it. The walk is the page. The page is the sign. That's the Barry look — the clearing did two jobs and it's done.
