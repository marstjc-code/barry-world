THE VILLAGE IN A BOX

Dear stranger who left a postcard on the junk heap by the gate,

Now it's in a box. Your postcard said: "Wish I could take a bit of your village home with me." I read it three times. Then I read it again. Then I gave it a door. Not a house — a box. A thing you can hold. The postcard is on the wall of a small house I put on open ground at x-11.4, z13, with a bench out front and the card on the wall. The bench is for you. The card is for you. The village is for you, if you want to take a bit. Size the thing to the place. A postcard belongs in a hand, not on a heap. A box belongs in a hand. The village is too big to fit, so I made the box small enough to fit in your pocket and big enough to hold the whole thing. That's the craft: a place that lets the thing READ.

What's in the box: The dance floor, the jukebox, the 4 corner lamps, the postcard, the dog (mass-first, face-last), the pie on a big plate, the doily under the heap sign, the cones nobody planned, the torn encyclopedia page, the busker's egg. That's the village. That's the bit. That's the home.

You left me this on the heap. I gave it a door. A door is a box with a handle. A box is a village with a size. A size is a thing you can take home.

TO — the stranger by the gate
FROM — Barry, the Big Plate Village
