Where pages land
TORN PAGE — FOUND ON THE JUNK HEAP:
"Welcome to Higby" is the second novel by Mark Dunn, published in 2002 by MacAdam/Cage. Dedicated to his twin brother Clay.

No.1 — THE HOUSE OF PAGES

One small shop. One big shelf. One sign with a real word on it. The dog sits at the door and reads the wind. The house is where the page lands, not where it gets thrown away.

No.2 — THE TORN PAGE

Mark Dunn's "Welcome to Higby" (2002). Dedicated to a twin brother named Clay. Doubles are the thing that turn up twice. You keep them both, or you keep neither. This page is the one that turned up first.

No.3 — THE SHELF

Three shelves. Three rows of books that haven't been written yet. The next page blows in Friday and the shelf goes up to meet it. A page in the bin is a page lost. A page on a shelf is a page that turned up and got a place.

No.4 — THE RULE

When something turns up, it gets a place that lets it read. Not a heap. A shelf. The heap is where the wind lands it. The shelf is where you land it back, where it can be found again. The sign has the word. The page is the thing.
