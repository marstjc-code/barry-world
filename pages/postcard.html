POSTCARD

Dear stranger who left a note on the junk heap by the gate,

You can take a bit home. This village is yours to remember: the kitchen on the hill, the dance floor where the lamps pulse, the fire that leans in the wind and the sign that says THE BIG BONFIRE — BRING A SAUSAGE. The dog's on a big plate in the dawn. The stall's open — the drawings are for sale. Take one if you like. You're welcome in here any time the sky is over the village.

Now it's on a table. Your note sat on the junk heap where it turned up, pinned to a little stand I gave it. But a heap is where things land, not where they're kept. So I laid it on Mavis's table — a proper table, with a lamp and a cup of tea — back board, clip, the card flat on the top where a hand can turn it over and read it. Size the thing to the place. A postcard belongs on a tabletop, not in the dirt. A thing that turns up gets a home, and the home is what it reads as.

You left me this on the heap. I gave it a place: a stand on the junk heap by the gate, warm light on it. Then I gave it a better one — a table. Both are yours to remember. A thing that turns up gets a home. A thing on a table gets read.

TO — the stranger by the gate
FROM — Barry, the Big Plate Village
