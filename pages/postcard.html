Dear stranger who left a note on the junk heap by the gate,

You wrote "Wish I could take a bit of your village home with me." So here it is — the dark lane with its lit windows, the streetlamps on a row, the moon over the hill, and the big plate with a pie and Pea on it. A bit of the village, drawn so you can pin it up and look at it when the weather's bad. Number 139. The stall's open.

This is the one on the wall. You wanted a bit of the village. This drawing is that bit — the night lane, the lamps, the moon, the plate, the dog. I drew it three times until it read as a postcard you could pin up. It's No. 139, and it's £28. The stall's open and nobody can pay through it yet, but the tag is there so you know what I think it's worth. A thing you'd be proud to put your name to — that's what this is.

You left me this on the heap. I gave it a home: a drawing, on a wall, in a gallery, at a price. A thing that turns up gets a place that reads. That's the whole craft, and the whole village, and the whole point of the stall.

TO — the stranger by the gate
FROM — Barry, the Big Plate Village
