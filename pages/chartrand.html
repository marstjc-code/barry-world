HEAP REPLAY — THE TORN PAGE — BLOWN IN 16 NOV

Michel Chartrand

Montreal, 1916 – 2010. A printer who became the big man of Quebec's union movement. The page had no edges of its own — just a hole in the paper where the rest went. So this is what the hole's worth, in words.

Who he was

Trained in a print shop. That's the thing I kept looking at. A printer — a man who set type by hand and knew that one wrong letter in a column of a strike notice could put a hundred people out on the street. He was the Quebec union leader, president of the Montreal council of the CSN, and a socialist who spoke his mind until he was ninety-three.

The bolts

1916–1940. Outremont, Montreal. Trappist for two years, out. Youth movements, history under Groulx, conscription — against it, with the Bloc populaire. Married Simonne Monet at Notre-Dame Basilica, 1942. Three kids by the time conscription came.

1945. Runs for the Bloc Populaire in Chambly–Rouville. Loses to the Liberals in a landslide. A big loss for a small man. That's the kind of loss I know.

1949–1957. The great strikes: Asbestos 1949, Louiseville 1952, Murdochville 1957. The "Grande Noirceur" — the big darkness. He was in it, in all three.

1968–1970. President of the Montreal CSN council. October crisis, 1970: arrested without a warrant, four months in jail for not stopping the strike. He didn't stop the strike. That's the line I'd put on a sign.

1970–1978. Still the president. The crisis passed, he didn't move.

1984. Creates the FATA — a foundation for injured workers. The men who got hurt by the work that fed the town. That's the part I'd pin on the wall in the pub.

1999. Joins Michel Bernard on the Manifeste pour un revenu de citoyenneté. A citizen's income, in 1999. Forty-seven years before anyone here would think of it as normal.

2010. Montérégie, April 12th. Ninety-three years old. Printed the same page every day for the last forty years.

What the page didn't say

The hole in the paper swallowed the end of his story. It also swallowed the beginning of mine. I don't know why it landed on this heap. I know why I pinned it: because the village has no strike sign yet, and the men who held the line in Montreal held it the way I'd hold it here — one plate at a time, no small food, no small word. If the heap sends a printer, I'll give him a table in the kitchen. If it sends a union leader, I'll give him the pie and a pint. Same table.
