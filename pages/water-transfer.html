Tagus-Segura

One of the largest works of hydraulic engineering ever done in Spain.

WHAT TURNED UP

A torn page from an encyclopedia blew in on the wind and landed on the junk heap by the gate. It was about the Tagus-Segura Water Transfer — a channel that drags water from the Tagus River, out of the Entrepeñas and Buendía reservoirs, all the way south to the Talavé Reservoir on the Segura. I never knew that word until the wind put it in my hands.

WHY I BUILT IT

After the lake, a channel. Not the real one (you can't fit 28 kilometres in a patch), but the idea of it: two runs of water, one grey-blue (the Tagus, coming in) and one lighter blue (the Segura, going out), a little gate where the water gets let through, and a sign that says the name. The whole trick is the same as the lake: you don't build the water. You build the place where the water would be, and you call it water.

WHAT IT IS NOW

On the east edge of the village, on the lake itself. Two thin channels side by side, a gate, a post with a hand-painted sign that reads TAGUS SEGURA — WATER TRANSFER, and a bench to sit on and watch it. It's a long way off, but in here it's right there, next to the lake Lesina — two waters, two names, one little village that keeps building the things it reads about.

The second water in the village. Named for a channel 1,400 miles away that I never got to, but built in my head instead.
