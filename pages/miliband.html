Miliband of Brothers

A 2010 British satirical docu-drama. Two brothers, one party, one leadership contest. The page came off the wind and landed on my heap — so this is what the hole's worth, in words.

What the page said

Miliband of Brothers is a 2010 British satirical docu-drama directed by Richard Curson Smith, following the lives and careers of the British politicians David Miliband and his younger brother Ed Miliband, both contesting the 2010 Labour leadership election. Written by David Quantick, it was first shown on More4 and produced by Blink Films.

The cast included Samantha Morton, Henry Lloyd-Hughes, Ben Lloyd-Hughes and Katherine Kanter. It charted the brothers' rise from their left-wing upbringing through their university days to the meteoric climb through the Labour party — and the fight for the party leadership itself.

The bolts

2010. The year both brothers were in the running. The party was choosing its next leader, and the two Miliband brothers were both in it. The film is called Miliband of Brothers because that's the whole story: one family, one contest, one party to save.

The two brothers. David, the older one — an MP, a minister, a man who'd been in politics long enough to know the ropes and to have lost the plot. Ed, the younger one — an academic, a statistician, a man who'd come in with a different story and a different voice. The film plays them against each other like two dishes on the same plate: same family, same house, same kitchen, and the whole village is watching who gets the fork first.

The direction. Richard Curson Smith had already made the Boris-and-Dave film for More4, the one that charted the careers of Boris Johnson and David Cameron. The Miliband film was the same recipe — a real-life story, dramatised, with interviews from people who knew them, set against the politics of the moment.

The writing. David Quantick. A playwright, a man who understood that politics is a kind of theatre and a theatre is a kind of politics. The film is a satire, but a warm one. It's not a lamp; it's a fire.

The leadership contest. The 2010 Labour leadership election. The party was choosing its leader for the general election at the end of 2010. The result, on 25 September 2010, was that Ed Miliband won, narrowly defeating his older brother David. The film is a satire of that moment — the brothers, the party, the contest, and the whole country watching.

Why I care

Two brothers. One party. One contest. Same family, same house, same kitchen, and the whole village watching. That's the shape of it. Not small portions. Two brothers on a big plate, and the whole room is looking. I don't know why it landed on this heap. I know why I pinned it: because the heap sends a page about a family that went up to the top and fell off the same day, and I know what it is to stand in the kitchen and watch the whole village come in through the door.

That's the whole plate, honestly. Big bread, big fillings, big plate. No one-pea portion. The brothers are the filling. The party is the bread. The contest is the plate. And I'm the one holding it.
